Module 7.3 — Weak-Area Review

This module has no new problems of its own — it's a structured process for turning the raw signal from Modules 7.1 and 7.2 (which patterns you struggled with, which follow-ups you fumbled) into a targeted return to the specific Module 6 sub-topics that need reinforcement, rather than re-reviewing everything evenly.

1. Why Targeted Review Beats Re-Reading Everything

Re-reading all of Module 6 from the start wastes time on topics you've already internalized, and — worse — gives a false sense of thoroughness without actually closing the gaps that matter. A weak-area review process directly uses your mock interview logs (Module 7.2, section 5) to decide where to spend time.

Rule of thumb: any sub-topic where pattern identification succeeded less than ~60% of the time on first attempt is a priority — this is a recognition gap, not just a syntax gap, and recognition gaps are what actually fail interviews (you can look up syntax; you can't look up "which pattern applies" mid-interview).

3. The Review Cycle for Each Weak Sub-Topic

  1. Re-read the relevant Module 6 sub-topic in full — not skimmed, since a gap in recognition often traces back to an incompletely internalized "why" (e.g., why a monotonic stack is O(n) despite the nested loop — Module 6.4).
  2. Re-solve the original practice questions for that sub-topic from memory, without looking at your previous solution.
  3. Solve 2–3 NEW problems on the same pattern (from an external source, since this course's practice sets are now familiar) to confirm the gap is actually closed, not just that you memorized the specific earlier problem's answer.
  4. Re-attempt one mixed practice set (Module 7.1) that included this pattern among others, to verify you can now recognize it correctly WITHOUT being told in advance which sub-topic it belongs to.

5. When a Weak Area Is Actually Fixed

Consider a sub-topic genuinely reinforced (not just "reviewed") when, across at least 3 NEW mixed-context problems:

  • The pattern is identified within the first few minutes, without hesitation.
  • The optimal (or near-optimal) time/space complexity is reached on the first attempt, not after a hint or a second pass.
  • Edge cases are anticipated proactively, before being pointed out.
  • The approach can be explained clearly out loud in under a minute.

If any of these still fail after one full review cycle, repeat the cycle rather than moving on — a pattern that's still shaky after one pass is a signal to spend more time, not less.

Practice Questions

This sub-topic's "practice" is the review cycle itself, applied to whichever Module 6 sub-topics your own mock interview logs identify as weak. There is no fixed problem set here by design — the entire point is that the problems worked on are determined by your own diagnostic data, not a generic list.
